Optimizing Altria's factory performance using generative AI.

Generative AI application to improve repair rates at Altria's manufacturing center by reducing the time to research/troubleshoot.

Team

Team of 15+ PMs, engineers, designers

The challenge

Six products used 14 shades of blue, 9 button styles and no shared accessibility baseline. Design-to-build handoff was slow and every team rebuilt the same components.

The solution

A token-based design system with accessible components in Figma and code, clear contribution rules and an adoption program that made switching easier than not.

The impact

Teams shipped faster with fewer inconsistencies, and the system became the default way to build in under a year.

01

Context

The company had grown from one product to six through acquisitions and fast hiring. Each team had its own Figma library and front-end stack.

Leadership wanted a cohesive brand. Designers wanted to stop redrawing buttons. Engineers wanted fewer one-off requests. Nobody wanted a mandate.

Pilot teams

Key insights

1

Inconsistency was a symptom

“We’d use a shared library if it had what we need.”

Teams diverged because the old library was incomplete, not because they wanted to.

2

Engineers are the real adopters

“If it isn’t in code, it doesn’t exist for us.”

Figma-only components never made it to production.

3

Contribution felt impossible

“I don’t know who to ask to add something.”

With no clear process, teams built one-offs instead of contributing back.

04

Defining the problem

How might we

How might we make the consistent, accessible choice the easiest choice for every designer and engineer, without slowing teams down?

Design principles

Tokens before components

Decisions live in shared tokens so every platform stays in sync.

Code is the source of truth

A component isn’t done until it ships in Storybook.

Open contribution

Anyone can propose; a clear process decides.

Reflection

What I’d carry into the next project:

A system is a product

Treating designers and engineers as users, with research and roadmaps, drove adoption.

Start with pilots, not decrees

Two successful teams persuaded the rest better than any memo.

Measure what you want to grow

The adoption dashboard turned migration into friendly competition.
