The challenge

Riders had to open the app, search a station and scroll through every line just to see their next train, often while walking down subway stairs with one free hand.

The solution

A context-aware home screen that predicts the rider’s likely trip, shows live departures up front and surfaces service changes before they become surprises.

The impact

01

Context

Transit Pulse had grown feature by feature for six years. Live arrivals, alerts, maps and fares each lived in separate tabs, and the home screen was a static station search.

Analytics showed 71% of sessions were the same two or three trips, repeated daily. Yet every session started from zero. The business goal was to win back ratings; the user goal was simply to stop missing trains.

02

Research

I combined qualitative and quantitative methods to understand behaviour, not just opinions.

18

1,240

Competitive audits

90 days

Analytics review

Session recordings and funnel data for the trip-lookup flow.

Key insights

1

Commuters check, not plan

“I know the route. I just need to know if I should run.”

Most sessions lasted under 10 seconds. People wanted a status check, not a planning tool.

2

Alerts arrive too late, or too often

“I muted the alerts after the third one about a line I never take.”

Notifications weren’t tied to a rider’s real routes, so riders tuned them out and missed the ones that mattered.

3

One hand, moving body

“I’m usually holding a coffee and going down stairs.”

Small tap targets near the top of the screen were nearly unusable in transit.

04

Defining the problem

How might we

How might we give commuters the one answer they need, “should I hurry?”, the moment they open the app, without asking them to do anything?

Design principles

Glance first

The most likely answer is visible without a single tap.

Earn the interruption

Only alert people about trips they actually take.

Thumb-friendly

Primary actions live in the bottom third of the screen.

05

Ideation & iteration

From rough concepts to tested high fidelity, each round was shaped by what we learned from real users.

What we learned in testingWhat we changed
Riders didn’t trust predictions they couldn’t editAdded a one-tap “not this trip” and visible “why we suggested this”
Red for delays read as errorsMoved to a calmer amber scale with plain-language labels
Countdown jitter caused anxietyShowed a stable range (“4–6 min”) when data was uncertain
06 · The solution

What we shipped.

A context-aware home screen that predicts the rider’s likely trip, shows live departures up front and surfaces service changes before they become surprises.

01

Predictive home

Learns a rider’s routine and opens straight to their likely trip, with live departures for both directions.

  • Time- and location-aware suggestions
  • Edit or dismiss in one tap
  • Works offline with last-known schedule
02

Route-aware alerts

Notifications are tied to saved trips and timed to when they’re useful, before leaving home, not on the platform.

  • Smart quiet hours
  • Plain-language disruption summaries
  • Suggested alternatives inline
03

Transfer mode

When a rider gets off, the app automatically switches to their next leg so there’s no search on the platform.

  • Auto-detects transfer stations
  • Walking time between platforms
  • Large, glanceable countdown
07

Testing & results

We ran two rounds of moderated testing (12 riders) and a Maze study (210 participants) before a staged rollout to 10% of users. The A/B test ran for four weeks.

−63%time to see next departure (14s → 5s)
+38%trip-planning completion rate
3.9 → 4.7★app-store rating after two releases
08

Reflection

What I’d carry into the next project:

Predictions need an escape hatch

Personalisation only felt helpful once people could see and correct it.

Test in the real context

Lab tests missed the one-hand problem entirely; ride-alongs caught it on day one.

Calm is a feature

Reducing visual alarm improved trust as much as faster data did.

Next, we’re exploring a lock-screen live activity and accessibility routing for step-free stations.
