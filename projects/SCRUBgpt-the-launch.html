The challenge

Booking a visit took 11 minutes on average and 39% of patients over 65 gave up, often calling the clinic instead and overloading front-desk staff.

The solution

A guided, one-question-per-screen booking flow with large type, plain language, a “call me instead” safety net and caregiver access.

The impact

Booking became faster and far more successful for older patients, and phone calls to clinics dropped noticeably.

11 → 4 minaverage time to book a visit

01

Context

The original flow was designed for speed by younger users: a dense single page with dropdowns, a tiny calendar and insurance fields up front.

CareLine’s clinic partners were paying for it in phone time. The goal was to make self-service booking genuinely work for older adults without slowing down everyone else.

02

Research

I combined qualitative and quantitative methods to understand behaviour, not just opinions.

14

In-home interviews

Visited patients aged 65–84, often with a family caregiver present.

22

Usability sessions

Tested the existing flow on patients’ own devices.

5

Clinic shadowing days

Listened to front-desk calls to map where self-service failed.

WCAG 2.2

Accessibility audit

Audited the flow with screen readers and contrast tools.

Key insights

1

Fear of doing it wrong

“If I press the wrong thing, will I lose my appointment?”

Anxiety, not ability, caused most drop-offs. People needed reassurance at every step.

2

Caregivers are co-pilots

“My daughter usually does this for me on her phone.”

Half of participants booked with help, but the product assumed a single user.

3

Insurance first is a wall

“I don’t have the card with me right now.”

Asking for insurance details first stopped people before they’d seen any value.

04

Defining the problem

How might we

How might we help older patients book care confidently on their own, while making it easy to ask for help when they want it?

Design principles

One thing at a time

Each screen asks a single, clearly worded question.

Always a way out

Every step offers “call me instead” and saves progress.

Accessible by default

Large type, high contrast and 48px targets everywhere.

05

Ideation & iteration

From rough concepts to tested high fidelity, each round was shaped by what we learned from real users.

What we learned in testingWhat we changed
Progress bar caused pressure (“I’m too slow”)Replaced with a friendly “Step 2 of 4” label
Patients feared the back buttonAdded explicit “Change answer” links and autosave
Video setup confused peopleAdded a 30-second test call before the visit
06 · The solution

What we shipped.

A guided, one-question-per-screen booking flow with large type, plain language, a “call me instead” safety net and caregiver access.

01

Guided booking

A calm, four-step flow that asks only what’s needed, in plain language, with insurance moved to the end.

  • One question per screen
  • Large, tappable time slots
  • Autosaves every answer
02

Caregiver access

Patients can invite a family member to book, join visits and receive reminders on their behalf.

  • Simple invite by text message
  • Clear permissions patients control
  • Shared visit summaries
03

Visit readiness check

A friendly test call checks camera, microphone and connection the day before, with a phone fallback.

  • Step-by-step device check
  • “Call me instead” at any time
  • Reminder with a large join button
07

Testing & results

We validated with 20 patients aged 65–84 across two rounds, plus 10 younger patients to confirm the flow didn’t slow anyone down. An accessibility partner verified WCAG 2.2 AA conformance before launch.
