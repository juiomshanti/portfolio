The challenge

Only 12% of customers visited the dashboard weekly. Owners exported data to spreadsheets or asked their accountant, and the product was losing them to simpler tools.

The solution

A question-led dashboard that answers “Can I pay my bills?” in plain language, forecasts cash 30 days ahead and flags what needs attention.

The impact

Owners came back weekly, found answers faster, and churn dropped in the cohort that used the new dashboard.

2.3×weekly active dashboard users
−60%time to answer key cash questions
−18%churn in first 90 days
01

Context

The existing dashboard was built by accountants, for accountants: profit and loss, balance sheets and twelve charts on one screen.

Most Ledger customers are owner-operators like bakers, contractors and salon owners. They aren’t finance experts and have about five minutes a week for the books.

02

Research

I combined qualitative and quantitative methods to understand behaviour, not just opinions.

16

Owner interviews

Spoke with owners across retail, trades and services.

8

Accountant interviews

Learned what accountants wished owners understood.

4,800

Usage analytics

Tracked which widgets were viewed, hovered or ignored.

40

Card-sort participants

Prioritised the questions owners care about most.

Key insights

1

Owners think in questions, not reports

“I don’t need a P&L. I need to know if I can make payroll Friday.”

Every interview surfaced the same 5 questions about near-term cash.

2

The future matters more than the past

“Last month is done. What’s coming?”

Owners cared about upcoming bills and income far more than historical charts.

3

Numbers without meaning cause anxiety

“Is that good or bad? I have no idea.”

Without context or comparisons, figures created stress rather than clarity.

04

Defining the problem

How might we

How might we help owner-operators understand their cash position and what to do about it in under a minute a week?

Design principles

Answer, then explain

Lead with a plain-language answer; details are one click away.

Look ahead

Default to the next 30 days, not the last 12 months.

Suggest the next step

Every insight comes with an action owners can take.

05

Ideation & iteration

From rough concepts to tested high fidelity, each round was shaped by what we learned from real users.

What we learned in testingWhat we changed

06 · The solution

What we shipped.

A question-led dashboard that answers “Can I pay my bills?” in plain language, forecasts cash 30 days ahead and flags what needs attention.

01

Cash at a glance

A plain-language headline answers “Am I okay this month?” with today’s balance and a 30-day outlook.

  • Plain-language summary
  • Comparisons to last month
  • Drill-down to transactions
02

30-day forecast

Combines scheduled bills, expected invoices and patterns to project cash, with the inputs visible.

  • Editable assumptions
  • Low-cash warnings ahead of time
  • What-if scenarios
03

Action queue

A short, prioritised list of what to do this week, like chasing a late invoice, with one-click follow-ups.

  • Top 3 actions only
  • One-click invoice reminders
  • Share with accountant
07

Testing & results

Three rounds of unmoderated tests (120 owners) and a 6-week beta with 300 businesses. We measured time-to-answer for five core questions before and after.

2.3×weekly active dashboard users
−60%time to answer key cash questions
−18%churn in first 90 days
08

Reflection

What I’d carry into the next project:

Co-design with data science early

The forecast only felt trustworthy because we designed the explanation together.

Less is genuinely more

Removing eight widgets drove more engagement than any new feature.

Language is the interface

Rewriting labels in owners’ own words was our highest-impact change.

Next: mobile weekly digests and a shared view for owners and accountants.
